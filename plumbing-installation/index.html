Plumbing Installation in Marina, CA

New fixtures, new lines and remodel rough-in, planned clearly and installed to code.

Call (855) 575-2890

Plumbing installation in Marina covers more than setting a fixture. It includes new supply and drain lines, moving a sink or toilet, adding a bathroom, and the rough-in plumbing that goes inside walls before drywall closes. We are licensed and insured, and we give you a clear price before work starts.

Good installation work is mostly planning. Pipe sizes, drain slopes, vent paths, shutoffs and access panels all get decided before anything is hidden. This page explains what each type of job involves, what published cost guides typically show, and what Marina homeowners and landlords should know before they start.

New lines and relocating fixtures

Moving a sink or toilet is the job that surprises owners. Drains need slope, and each fixture needs a vent so the trap does not siphon. The California Plumbing Code, part of the California Building Standards Code, sets limits on how far a trap can sit from its vent, so a fixture cannot be placed just anywhere.

Slab homes and second-floor bathrooms add limits of their own. A slab may need cutting, and a new drain on an upper floor needs framing space. We walk the site, mark the route and tell you if a move is practical before you buy materials.

Remodel rough-in: the stage before the walls close

  1. Measure and mark

    We confirm fixture positions, heights and clearances with the cabinets, tub and vanity you chose.

  2. Run the lines

    Supply, drain and vent lines go into open framing with shutoffs placed where you can reach them.

  3. Pressure test

    Before anything is covered, the lines are tested for leaks.

  4. Inspection

    Where a permit is required, the city inspects the rough-in before drywall. The Building Division schedules inspections by appointment, at least 24 hours ahead.

  5. Trim out

    After walls and tile, we set faucets, toilets and fixtures and test every one.

Order fixtures early. The rough-in depends on the actual model, because a toilet or tub that arrives late can force lines to be moved.

Licensing, permits and who can do the work

California requires a contractor's license for projects of $1,000 or more in labor and materials, and for any job that needs a building permit. Plumbing falls under the C-36 classification. You can check any contractor's license status for free on the Contractors State License Board site, and we encourage that for any trade.

The City of Marina requires contractors to hold a city business license before a permit is issued. For permit questions, the city's Building Division is the source. Do not assume a fixture swap is exempt from rules. Ask first.

We are licensed and insured, and we stay in contact with you on timing, since inspections are scheduled by appointment and not on demand.

Marina conditions that shape an install

Marina Coast Water District owns the meter and service connection. Pipes beyond the meter are yours, so any new line you add is also yours to maintain. Under MCWD code, the owner also maintains the side sewer, which matters if a new bathroom adds load to the lateral.

MCWD reported average hardness near 10.5 grains per gallon in 2025. New fixtures with fine screens and valves, such as filtered faucets and tankless units, benefit from planning for scale. A water softener is worth discussing before a major install.

State environmental records describe the dunes around Marina as loose, unconsolidated sand. Trenches in sandy ground need careful bedding and backfill so new drains keep their slope over time.

Marina has more renters than owners, so landlords often run these projects. We can work with a property manager on scheduling and access.

Before we arrive

  • Clear the area under sinks, around the toilet and near the water heater.
  • Measure the space and note the make and model of any fixture you bought.
  • Locate the main shutoff and confirm it turns.
  • Photograph the existing plumbing and any access panels.
  • Keep pets and children out of the work area.
  • Tell us about any recent leaks, slow drains or low pressure.
