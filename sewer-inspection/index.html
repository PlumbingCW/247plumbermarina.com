Sewer Camera Inspection in Marina, CA

A camera on a flexible cable shows what is inside your sewer line, so repairs are based on what is really there.

Call (855) 575-2890

Most sewer problems are hidden underground, and guessing is expensive. A sewer camera inspection in Marina, CA puts a waterproof camera inside the line and shows you roots, cracks, offsets, belly sections and buildup. It is a planned visit, scheduled around you, and you get a clear price before work starts.

This page is for the calm kind of call: buying a house, chasing a slow drain that keeps returning, or checking the lateral before a remodel. If sewage is already coming up in the house, that is a backup, and our sewer line cleaning page explains the faster route.

When a camera inspection makes sense

  • You are buying a home and want to know the condition of the lateral before closing.
  • A drain clears after cleaning and then slows again within weeks.
  • Several fixtures gurgle or the lowest drain in the house is slow.
  • You smell sewer gas near a cleanout, in a yard or in a crawl space.
  • You plan to plant trees, pour concrete or add a room over or near the line.
  • A wet or sunken patch has appeared in the yard and you do not know why.

Property owners decide these checks. Under Marina Coast Water District code, the owner maintains the side sewer that serves the property. The district also encourages owners to inspect their laterals and keep them clear, so a scheduled look is in line with its guidance.

What the camera shows

The camera head has its own light and records as it moves. We feed it from a cleanout or a pulled fixture, and you watch the screen or receive the recording. Distance is tracked so any problem can be located along the line.

Home buyers and landlords

Marina has more renters than owners, based on a 42.9 percent owner-occupied rate in recent Census data. For landlords, a recorded camera run gives a clear baseline of lateral condition, and for buyers, it can bring a hidden defect into a price discussion before closing.

A recording also helps if a repair is needed later, since a repair contractor, the owner and any property manager can all see the same video. Keep a copy with your closing papers. If you are comparing it with a general home inspection, note that a general inspector usually does not scope the underground line.

How the visit works

  1. Pick a time

    Call (855) 575-2890 to set a convenient window. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

  2. Find access

    We locate a cleanout or choose the best fixture to enter through. Code calls for cleanouts at bends of 45 degrees or more, so many homes have one.

  3. Clean if needed

    If grease or debris blocks the view, we clear a path first. That part is quoted before we do it.

  4. Scope the line

    We run the camera to the district connection, note problems and distances, and record the run.

  5. Review together

    We explain what we saw, which findings need action and which can be monitored.

What an inspection typically costs

Published cost guides typically show national figures for camera inspection. HomeAdvisor, Angi and This Old House place it roughly between $100 and $500, and one HomeAdvisor page on hydro jetting gives a wider $275 to $1,800 for sewer inspections. Those numbers are not Marina quotes.

Price moves with line length, access and whether cleaning must happen first. A home with a good cleanout near the street is simpler than one that needs a toilet pulled. We give a clear price before work starts, and we tell you if the inspection is likely to find nothing.

After the video: your repair choices

Many inspections end with no repair at all, just a record that the line is sound. When there is a problem, the video points to the least invasive fix. A grease coating may only need hydro jetting. A single crack or bad joint may need a spot repair. A longer failure may need lining or replacement, covered on our sewer line repair page.

Worth knowing

Old cast iron and fiber pipe can be fragile. A camera first tells us if heavy cleaning is safe, which can save a line from damage.

Local ground and pipe age in Marina

Marina sits in an area of loose, unconsolidated dune sand, according to a state environmental report on the Monterey Bay coast. Pipes bedded in sand rely on good support, and joints can drift if the bedding washes out. A camera can reveal sags that point to settled ground.

Housing here ranges from older central neighborhoods to newer subdivisions and former Fort Ord housing. We make no assumptions about pipe material from the address alone. The camera tells us what was installed. If you need help clearing a drain first, our drain cleaning page explains that service.
