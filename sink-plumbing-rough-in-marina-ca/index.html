Sink Plumbing Rough-In Dimensions and Heights in Marina, CA

The numbers a sink rough-in usually follows, and the handoffs that keep a kitchen from being opened up twice.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Marina 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026.

Sink plumbing rough-in is the water and drain piping set inside the wall before the drywall and cabinets go in. In most kitchens the hot and cold stubs sit about 20 to 24 inches above the finished floor, and the drain stub sits about 16 to 20 inches above it. Those heights are commonly shown in retailer and trade guides. They are not code numbers, so your fixture sheet and your cabinet drawings win.

This guide is for homeowners in Marina, CA who are remodeling, adding a sink, or moving one. It explains each dimension, draws the wall in words, and splits the job into what a plumber sets and what a cabinet maker needs to know. If the wall is already open and you want a second set of eyes, call (855) 575-2890.

What a rough-in actually is

A rough-in is the hidden stage of a plumbing job. The plumber bores holes through studs, runs supply lines and a drain, and leaves short stubs where the faucet and trap will connect later. Nothing visible is finished yet. The sink, faucet and trim come after the walls are closed.

The reason this stage matters is simple. Once drywall and cabinets are installed, a wrong stub means cutting open finished work. Getting five or six measurements right on day one is far cheaper than patching a wall after tile is set.

A rough-in also has to satisfy the fixture you chose. A deep farmhouse sink, a drop-in bowl and a bar sink do not need the same drain height, so the sink model should be known before pipe is cut.

Typical heights and offsets at a glance

The table below uses figures a major retailer guide commonly shows for a kitchen sink. Treat them as starting points, then match them to the sink and cabinet you actually bought.

Common kitchen sink rough-in figures from a national retailer guide. These are not code requirements.
ItemCommonly shown figureWhy it matters
Hot and cold supply stubsAbout 20 to 24 inches above finished floor, 23 most commonLeaves room for the shutoff valves and supply lines under the bowl
Supply spacingHot about 4 inches left of center, cold about 4 inches rightPuts the stubs about 8 inches apart so the valves do not crowd each other
Drain stubAbout 16 to 20 inches above finished floor, 18 most commonSets how steep the trap can drop and where the tailpiece lands
Drain sizeAt least 1-1/2 inches for a single-bowl sinkSmaller pipe clogs sooner and may fail inspection
Trap depthP-trap no more than about 24 inches below the sink drainA trap that hangs too low stops the sink from draining well

Center means the centerline of the sink base, not the wall. If the sink sits off-center in a long run of cabinets, measure from the cabinet drawing and move the stubs to match.

The wall drawn in words

Picture the wall behind the sink base, standing in front of it. The floor is at the bottom and the countertop line is about 36 inches up. Hidden behind the cabinet back are three stubs.

  1. Left of center, about 23 inches up, is the hot water stub, capped or fitted with a shutoff.
  2. Right of center, at the same height, is the cold water stub, about 8 inches from the hot one.
  3. Dead center, lower down at about 18 inches, is the drain stub, usually a 1-1/2 inch or 2 inch pipe coming out of the wall or up from the floor.

A dishwasher, if there is one, adds a separate hot supply and a drain connection that ties into the sink drain above the trap. Those get marked on the same drawing so nobody guesses later.

Who owns each handoff on a sink rough-in.

The most common gap is the cabinet back. A solid panel with no opening forces a cut on site, and a cut in the wrong place can hit a stub. Agree on the opening before the cabinets arrive.

Trap arm length and venting

Every sink drain needs a vent so the water leaves quietly and the trap seal stays in place. The distance from the trap to the vent is called the trap arm, and the California Plumbing Code publishes a table of maximum lengths by pipe size. As that table is commonly published, a 1-1/2 inch drain allows a trap arm up to about 42 inches, and a 2 inch drain allows up to about 60 inches.

Retailer guides sometimes give a single vent distance such as 5 feet. Use the code table through your plumber instead, because the right answer depends on pipe size and slope. Ask which edition of the code applies on your permit, since the adopted edition is a local decision.

Worth knowing

An island sink is the case that usually needs a special vent layout. Plan it before the floor is poured or the cabinets are set, because the vent route is hard to add later.

Mistakes that cause callbacks

  • Setting stubs before the sink model is chosen, then finding the drain sits too high or too low.
  • Leaving no access panel or valve opening in the cabinet back.
  • Placing the drain so far off center that the trap kinks or must reach across a cabinet divider.
  • Forgetting the dishwasher supply and drain until the cabinets are in.
  • Running the supply stubs too close to the cabinet floor, which crowds the shutoff valves.
  • Skipping a pressure test, so a pinhole leak shows up inside a closed wall.

A short checklist before the drywall closes

  1. Lock the fixtures

    Choose the sink, faucet, disposal and dishwasher first, and keep the spec sheets and cabinet drawing in one place.

  2. Mark the wall

    Mark the sink centerline and each stub height on the studs so everyone reads the same numbers.

  3. Pressure test

    Have the supply lines tested and the drain checked for slope and leaks while the wall is still open.

  4. Photograph the open wall

    Take photos with a tape measure in frame. They show where pipes run if you cut into that wall years later.

Marina notes: permits, inspections and water district rules

The City of Marina Building Division is at 209 Cypress Avenue and takes calls at (831) 884-1241. Its published hours are Monday through Friday, 8:00 am to 4:00 pm, closed from noon to 1:00 pm. For a sink move, that call is the fastest way to learn whether your job needs a permit, because the city's pages do not spell out plumbing permit rules.

The division says inspections are by appointment only and must be scheduled at least 24 hours ahead, in a morning or afternoon window. For you, that means the open-wall stage needs a day of lead time. Do not plan to close drywall the same day you call. The city also requires contractors to hold a City of Marina business license before a permit is issued, so ask for it when you hire.

Marina's municipal code includes a Plumbing Code chapter, Chapter 15.24, which was replaced by an ordinance in November 2022. The edition year was not visible in the text we read, so ask the division which edition governs your permit before the plumber sets trap arm lengths.

Marina Coast Water District code puts pipes and fixtures beyond the meter on the customer, and the district's rules call for leaks to be repaired within 72 hours. A new sink line is your responsibility from day one. The district also offers free 1.5 gallon per minute faucet aerators at (831) 883-5905, which matter when you choose a faucet for the new sink.
