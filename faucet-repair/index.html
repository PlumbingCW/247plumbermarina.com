Faucets

Emergency Faucet Repair in Marina, CA

A faucet that sprays, will not shut off or floods the cabinet gets a fast repair, day or night.

Call (855) 575-2890

A faucet that will not shut off, or a supply line that bursts under a sink, can put water on the floor in minutes. Our emergency faucet repair in Marina runs 24/7, so a call at midnight gets the same attention as one at noon. While you wait, close the angle stop under the sink, or the main shutoff if the stop is stuck, and empty the cabinet so you can see where the water comes from.

Most faucet emergencies are one of four things: a failed cartridge, a split supply hose, a corroded valve, or a loose connection under the sink. We repair or replace the faucet, explain the choice and give a clear price before work starts. Same-day appointments are offered when available.

Water spraying or pooling? Steps to take first

  1. Turn off the angle stop under the sink. Both handles turn clockwise, one for hot and one for cold.
  2. If a stop will not turn, close the main shutoff at the house or at the meter box on your side of the line.
  3. Move everything out of the cabinet and mop standing water so you can find the source.
  4. Put a bucket under any drip and keep towels around the base of the sink.
  5. Keep wet hands away from outlets, and switch off a circuit if water is reaching one.

Do not force a stop valve that is crusted shut. A snapped stem makes the leak worse, and we can replace the stop as part of the repair.

Many leaks that look like a faucet problem start at the supply line. A hose with a hairline split can sit dry for months and then fail on a Friday night.

Kitchen, bathroom, tub and outdoor faucets

Kitchen faucets

Pull-down sprayers add hoses and weights under the sink that can tangle or wear through. A leak at the sprayer head or under the sink often means a hose replacement, not a new faucet.

Bathroom and tub faucets

Two-handle bathroom faucets need new seats or washers. Tub spouts and diverters that leak behind the wall are different. If you see a stain on the other side of a bathroom wall, tell us so we can check for a hidden leak. Our leak detection work covers that.

Outdoor spigots

A spigot that drips at the stem usually needs a new packing or washer. A spigot with water inside the wall or crawl space is more serious and should be shut off at the valve inside.

Cartridge, repair or full replacement

A repair makes sense when the faucet body is sound and only the cartridge, seat or hose has failed. A new faucet is the better call when the body is corroded, parts are no longer made, or the finish is peeling. We show you the options and the clear price before work starts.

Hard water and low pressure at the tap

Marina Coast Water District reported average hardness of about 10.5 grains per gallon in 2025, with some readings near 19. Minerals collect in aerators, cartridges and valve seats. The result is a spray that splits sideways, a slow trickle, or a handle that grinds.

MCWD hands out free faucet aerators through its conservation program, which is a low-cost way to replace a clogged one. If flow is weak at every tap, the cause is not the aerator. It may be a pressure regulator, a partly closed valve or old pipe, and we can trace it.

If hot water is the problem, a faucet that runs cold or lukewarm may point to the heater. See water heater repair for that.

Who pays and what the district expects

Under MCWD code, the district owns the meter and the service connection. Everything past the meter, including faucets, supply lines and shutoffs, belongs to the customer. District rules also say leaks and defects must be repaired within 72 hours.

Renters make up most of Marina, since about 43 percent of homes are owner-occupied. Tell your landlord promptly in writing. Where a lease says tenants arrange repairs, we can quote the work and explain it clearly.

A credit for lost water is possible only in limited cases and is up to the district. A fast repair is the sure way to limit the cost.

What happens on the visit

  1. Stop the water

    We close the supply, dry the area and confirm what is actually leaking.

  2. Find the cause

    We check the cartridge, the lines, the stops and the sink connections, not just the part you can see.

  3. Explain the options

    You hear what repair or replacement involves and the clear price before work starts.

  4. Fix and test

    We repair or replace, run both hot and cold, and look under the sink for any weeping joint.
