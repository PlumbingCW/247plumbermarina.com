75-Gallon Water Heater Installed Cost in Marina, CA

Published price ranges for large tank heaters, and the bracing, space and venting questions that come with them.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Marina 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026.

A 75-gallon water heater installed costs about $1,500 to $3,400 in a January 2026 national cost guide, with an average near $2,100. That guide splits it by energy source, and the spread is wide. If you are pricing a 75-gallon water heater in Marina, CA, expect your own number to depend on fuel, venting and access.

A tank this size is a bigger job than a standard 40 or 50 gallon unit. It needs more room, a sound floor, correct earthquake strapping and often a more careful vent. Below is the cost by size and fuel, then the points that decide whether the quote holds up.

What moves a 75-gallon price

Labor made up roughly $250 to $1,700 in the guide, and that spread says most of the story. A straight swap in an open garage is near the bottom. A heater in a tight closet that needs new venting is near the top.

  • Fuel: electric is the lowest in the guide, gas and propane cost more, and heat pump models cost the most.
  • Venting: gas units need a vent that matches the new heater.
  • Removal and disposal of the old tank, listed at $50 to $150.
  • An expansion tank, listed at $150 to $400.
  • Permits and inspections, which vary by city.
  • Access: stairs, attics and narrow doors add labor.

Ask the plumber to write each of these as its own line, so you can see what is included and what is not. Our clear price before work starts covers that.

Space, weight and venting for a big tank

A 75-gallon tank is taller and wider than the heater it likely replaces. Before you order, measure the closet, the door and the ceiling height, and compare against the spec sheet for the model. Leave clearance for the gas controls, the relief valve and service access.

Weight matters too. Water weighs about 8 pounds per gallon, so a full 75-gallon tank holds roughly 600 pounds of water, before the weight of the tank. The floor, the platform and the straps all carry that load.

A gas heater needs a vent path that suits its type, and the plumber should confirm combustion air and clearances. A guide on 75-gallon units lists venting as one of the cost drivers for gas models. If the old flue cannot be reused, price in the new vent.

Relief valve discharge

The temperature and pressure relief valve needs a discharge pipe. A California city bulletin summarizing the plumbing code says it runs to the outside or an approved receptor, ends 6 inches to 2 feet above grade, has no shutoff valve and no trap. Your plumber should confirm what applies in Marina.

Earthquake bracing is required

California Health and Safety Code section 19211 requires new and replacement water heaters, and existing residential ones, to be braced, anchored or strapped against falling or horizontal movement in an earthquake. The law covers standard heaters up to 120 gallons that have a pre-engineered strapping kit available, so a 75-gallon unit falls inside it.

A city plumbing bulletin that cites the plumbing code describes two straps, one in the upper third of the tank and one in the lower third, with the lower strap at least 4 inches above the controls. The straps tie to the wall framing and not to drywall alone. A heavy tank needs solid anchoring.

The seller of a home must also certify in writing that the heater is braced. If you are selling, ask the plumber to note the strapping on the invoice.

Permits, inspection and the plumber's license

We could not confirm the City of Marina's rule for water heater permits on its public pages, though other California cities require one for every replacement. Call the Building Division at (831) 884-1241. It is open Monday through Friday and takes inspection appointments in advance.

The city's page says a contractor must hold a City of Marina business license before a permit is issued. It also says inspections are by appointment, at least 24 hours ahead, in a morning or afternoon window. If a permit applies, build that wait into your plan.

In California a job that needs a permit falls outside the $1,000 minor work exemption, so a plumber should hold a state license. Check it with the Contractors State License Board at 1-800-321-CSLB. See our water heater replacement and water heater installation pages for the process.

Is 75 gallons the right size

A bigger tank gives you more hot water at once, but it does not make hot water unlimited. A large tank also keeps more water heated while it waits. If your household rarely needs that volume, a smaller tank may cost less to buy and run.

SituationDirection to consider
Large household with back-to-back showersA 75-gallon tank may fit
Small home, one or two peopleA smaller tank usually costs less
Limited closet spaceCheck dimensions before choosing size
Hot water needed with little floor spaceAsk about a tankless water heater

Your plumber can size the heater to the number of bathrooms, fixtures and people in your home.

Marina water, rentals and the permit desk

Marina Coast Water District's 2025 report put average hardness at 180.3 mg/L, or 10.54 grains per gallon, with readings up to about 19 gpg. The district recommends flushing the water heater regularly according to the manufacturer. For a 75-gallon tank, that means more volume to drain and more room for sediment, so ask for a drain valve you can reach.

Census QuickFacts for Marina show 42.9 percent of housing is owner-occupied, so most households rent. Owners of duplexes and small apartment buildings often choose larger tanks for shared use. If you manage rentals, keep the invoice that shows strapping, since the law requires proof of bracing when a property is sold.

Marina is in earthquake country. A full tank that tips can break gas and water lines. Strapping is the cheapest protection in this whole job.
