How to pick the right service

Plumbing problems get named by symptom, not by trade term. Someone says the kitchen sink is slow, and the real cause might be a grease buildup in the branch line, a worn disposal, or a main line problem that also affects the tub. The guide below links each common symptom to the service most likely to fix it.

Urgent problems come first

Some calls cannot wait for the next opening. Active flooding, sewage coming up a floor drain and a water heater leaking at the base are the usual ones. For those, close the main water valve, stop using plumbing fixtures and call. A burst pipe repair or water service line repair is treated as urgent from the first ring.

Smell gas? Do not call a plumber first

Leave the building, call 911 from outside, then call the gas utility. For PG&E, that is 1-800-743-5000. We can help with the plumbing side once the building is declared safe.

Planned work and upgrades

Not everything is an emergency. If you are replacing an aging water heater, adding a fixture or planning to replace old supply lines, you have time to compare options. Our pages on water heater installation, tankless water heaters, repiping and plumbing installation cover the choices and trade-offs.

Marina's water is moderately hard to hard, per the water district's 2025 report, so a water softener or filtration setup is worth discussing alongside any new heater. Older homes also benefit from a sewer inspection before a purchase or before you plant over a lateral.

For restaurants, shops and offices

Businesses need quick answers, because a down restroom or a plugged kitchen line costs sales. Our commercial plumbing page describes how we handle service calls for food service, retail and light industrial sites. Restaurants should know that grease interceptors have their own pumping schedule under local wastewater rules.
