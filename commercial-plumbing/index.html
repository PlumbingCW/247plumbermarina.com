Plumbing emergency at your business? Do this first

  1. Stop the source

    Close the local shutoff or the building main if you cannot find the leak. Know where the main is before you need it.

  2. Protect people and power

    Keep staff off wet floors near equipment. Switch off power to flooded outlets or machines if it is safe to reach the panel.

  3. Limit the damage

    Move stock, paper and electronics above the waterline. Do not run dishwashers, sinks or floor drains that are backing up.

  4. Document it

    Take photos for your insurance company before cleanup starts. Policies commonly separate sudden bursts from gradual leaks, so check yours.

  5. Call us

    Tell us the type of business, what is overflowing and whether the issue is one fixture or the whole building.

If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 and then the gas utility. We do not give a response time for gas, because the first step is always to get out safely.

Backflow testing in MCWD's service area

Marina Coast Water District requires backflow prevention assemblies to be installed where a hazard is identified. The district says assemblies must be tested annually, and also after installation, repair or a leak. Only testers certified by the American Water Works Association may do the testing, and MCWD publishes a list of approved testers.

According to MCWD code, the owner or water user pays for testing, repair and compliance. That makes a failed test a business cost, not a district cost. Irrigation systems, fire lines and kitchens with certain equipment can all have assemblies.

What we cover

Testing goes to an AWWA-certified tester on the district's list. If an assembly fails, repair or replacement is plumbing work, and we can handle that part. Keep the annual test on your calendar, since a lapse can create problems with the district.

Grease traps and FOG for food service

Restaurants and other food service businesses must keep fats, oils and grease out of the sewer. MCWD runs a FOG program with a regional partner, and its code requires grease traps or interceptors to be pumped regularly. Ask MCWD for the current interval and the rules for your size of unit, because grease rules can change.

In practice, the rule is simple. A full trap lets grease pass into the lateral, where it hardens and blocks the line. Keep pump-out records, since the district can ask to see them.

  • Scrape plates dry before washing.
  • Never pour fats, oils or grease down a sink or disposal.
  • Do not rely on hot water or soap. Grease reforms and solidifies in the pipe.
  • Schedule pump-outs with a licensed hauler.
  • Call us early if sinks drain slowly, since the trap may be full.

Floor drains, lift pumps and storm drains

Floor drains in kitchens, restrooms and mechanical rooms back up when the line is blocked or when a trap dries out. A lift pump or sump that fails during a rain event can flood a basement or low room quickly. We repair and replace these, and we find out why they failed.

Storm drains need a short word. They are meant for rainwater only. Mop water, wash water and grease should go to the sanitary sewer, not to a parking-lot inlet. Cleaning out a clogged storm inlet or catch basin on your property is routine maintenance, and we can do it before the wet season.

Marina's business districts and their pipes

Central Marina has older commercial strips along Del Monte Boulevard, while newer retail and housing sit around The Dunes on Monterey Bay. Older buildings may have mixed pipe materials from earlier remodels, and newer buildings may have tighter mechanical spaces. Either way, we trace the route before cutting.

Soil here is loose dune sand, so buried drains and laterals depend on good bedding. The property owner maintains the side sewer under district code, so a blocked lateral is the owner's repair, and tenants should tell the landlord promptly.
