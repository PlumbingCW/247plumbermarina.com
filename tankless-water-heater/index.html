How a tankless heater works at your tap

When a hot tap opens, flow sensors trigger a burner or electric elements. Water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves at the set temperature. When the tap closes, the unit shuts off. Nothing is stored, so standby heat loss is gone.

The tradeoff is capacity. A tankless unit has a maximum flow in gallons per minute at a given temperature rise. Run a shower, a dishwasher and a laundry load together and a small unit may not keep up. That is why sizing matters more than brand.

Safety steps if a tankless unit fails

If you smell gas near a tankless unit, step outside, call 911 from a safe distance, and then contact PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.

  • Close the water and gas valves on the unit only if they are easy to reach and there is no gas odor.
  • Do not remove the vent or the cover to look inside.
  • Write down any error code on the display so you can tell the plumber.

Installing a tankless unit: gas, venting and power

Most tankless installs involve more than swapping the heater. Gas models often need a larger gas line than a tank heater, because the burner is much stronger. They also need a sealed or direct vent run, and the vent route can change the price.

Electric models draw large amounts of current and may need new breakers or a service panel upgrade. Point-of-use units are smaller and serve one fixture, such as a distant bathroom. For a whole-house comparison with storage tanks, see water heater installation.

  • Gas line size checked against the unit's input rating.
  • Vent material, route and termination planned before purchase.
  • Electrical load and panel space confirmed for electric models.
  • Condensate drain for high-efficiency gas units.
  • Pressure relief protection fitted per the maker's instructions.
  • Permit and inspection questions settled with the City of Marina Building Division at (831) 884-1241.

We do not state a Marina permit rule without confirming it. The Building Division will tell you what applies to your address, including any questions about fuel type.

Flushing and descaling with Marina Coast Water District water

Marina Coast Water District's 2025 water quality report shows total hardness averaging about 10.5 grains per gallon, with a range of 4.9 to 19.3. Hardness is not a regulated drinking water standard, but it does matter for heaters. The district suggests regular flushing of water heaters, following the manufacturer's guidance, to limit mineral buildup.

In a tankless unit, minerals coat the narrow passages of the heat exchanger. Symptoms include longer waits for hot water, fluctuating temperature, error codes and reduced flow. A flush pushes a descaling solution through the unit and then rinses it clean.

Maker instructions usually include isolation valves with service ports, which make the flush simple. If your unit lacks them, we can add them. A softener upstream can reduce scale at the source, and our water softener and filtration page covers when that makes sense.

Repairing a tankless heater: codes, cold bursts and leaks

Tankless units report faults through error codes on the display or remote panel. The code tells us which sensor, igniter, fan or flame circuit the unit flagged. Share the code when you call and we can plan the visit.
