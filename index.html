The first ten minutes of a plumbing emergency

What you do before a plumber arrives often decides how much damage there is. Most of it takes a minute and no tools. Learn where your shutoffs are now, while nothing is wrong.

  • Close the main water valve, usually near the meter, where the line enters the house, or by the water heater.
  • Stop using sinks, tubs, showers and toilets if a drain is backing up.
  • Switch off the water heater if the tank is leaking, and keep people away from wet outlets and appliances.
  • Move rugs, boxes and electronics off the floor. Take photos of damage for your records.
  • Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us what you see, hear and smell.

Gas smell: leave first

If you smell natural gas, do not look for the source. Leave the building, call 911 from a safe distance, then call the gas utility. For PG&E that number is 1-800-743-5000. A plumbing call comes after that.

Marina places and what they mean for your pipes

Marina sits on the Monterey Bay shoreline, and the ground under it is mostly sand. A state environmental report describes the area's dunes as unconsolidated, highly erodible sand. Sandy soil drains well, but it can also shift around buried lines, so we look closely at older yard and sewer runs.

Marina State Beach is at the end of Reservation Road, and a day there often ends with sandy gear at an outdoor spigot or shower drain. Sand and grit can wear hose bibs and collect in traps, so a slow outdoor drain is worth a look before it backs up.

Marina Dunes Preserve and Fort Ord Dunes State Park frame the north and south edges of town. A home on a sandy lot near open dunes can hide trouble, because an exterior water leak can disappear into the ground without showing at the surface. A meter that keeps turning after you shut everything off is the clue.

Locke-Paddon Wetland Community Park sits just off Reservation Road next to the Marina Library at 190 Seaside Circle. Homes near low, damp ground like this are better off with a working cleanout on your sewer line, so a backup can be cleared without digging.

CSUMB has a physical campus address of 5108 Fourth Ave in Marina, though its mailing address uses Seaside. Shared rentals with many people on one set of fixtures are where toilet clogs and disposal jams tend to start. Landlords and tenants can both call us for those.

Preston Park was built in 1989 for military families and is near Reservation Road and Imjin Road. Housing from that era can have aging supply lines and original water heaters, so a leak or a tank at the end of its life is a fair thing to expect there.

Reservation Road, Del Monte Boulevard and Highway 1 are how most calls reach us. Del Monte Boulevard is the main commercial strip through central Marina, so restaurants and shops along it are the usual source of grease clogs and restroom backups.

We also take calls from the former Fort Ord area, and from nearby Sand City, Del Rey Oaks and Monterey, and from north county communities such as Prunedale. Neighbors have their own pages: see our Seaside plumber page and our Castroville plumber page.

Marina water: hard enough to notice

Marina Coast Water District pumps groundwater from wells in the Salinas Valley basin and treats it with chlorine. Its 2025 report lists average hardness at 10.54 grains per gallon, with readings up to about 19. That is moderately hard to hard water.

Hard water leaves scale inside water heaters and on faucet screens. The district itself suggests flushing your water heater on the manufacturer's schedule. If scale has already built up, a water heater repair visit may be enough, or a water softener and filtration setup may be the longer-term answer.
