Service line leak: what to do first

  1. Close the main shutoff at the house. If it will not turn, do not force it.
  2. Look at the meter box. If water is welling up around the meter itself, call the Marina Coast Water District 24-hour line at (831) 883-5900.
  3. Photograph wet soil, sunken ground or water at the curb.
  4. Keep children and pets away from soft or sunken patches near the line.
  5. Call (855) 575-2890 for the pipe between the meter and the house.

If the pipe let go suddenly and water is pouring, our burst pipe repair page has shutoff steps. If you smell gas near the work area, leave, call 911 once you are outside and away from the building, then reach PG&E at 1-800-743-5000.

Who owns what between the street and your house

Under the district's code, the water district owns its meters and the service connection, even where the connection crosses private property. It maintains the connection and the meter box at its own expense. The district does not maintain lines beyond the end of its service connection. Customers are responsible for pipes and fixtures on the customer side of the meter.

Signs the line to your house is failing

Most people notice a bill first. A jump in usage with nobody home is among the most common reasons people call about service lines. Other signs show up outdoors.

  • Wet or soft ground, or a dip in the yard or driveway.
  • Pressure that drops at every fixture.
  • A meter that moves while all fixtures are off.
  • A hissing sound near the meter or the foundation.
  • A green or lush strip in an otherwise dry yard.

The meter test works well here. Turn everything off, record the dial, close the house main, and check again. A moving meter with the main closed points to the pipe between the meter and the house. Our leak detection service can locate it without digging the yard.

Repair or replace: trench and trenchless

A small leak in sound pipe may need a short repair. A pipe that has failed more than once usually calls for a longer replacement, and failures throughout the house may point to repiping.
