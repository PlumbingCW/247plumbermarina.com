Repiping

Whole-House Repiping in Marina, CA

Replace worn supply lines once, instead of chasing leaks one wall at a time.

Repiping means replacing the water supply lines inside a house, and for many Marina, CA owners it comes up after the third or fourth leak. This page explains how to tell whether repair or repiping makes sense, how the materials compare, what published cost guides show, and what the work involves.

We look at your actual pipes first, then explain the choices in plain words. You get a clear price before work starts. Licensed and insured plumbers serve Marina, Seaside, Castroville and nearby Monterey County.

Warning signs your supply pipes are wearing out

One leak does not mean the whole house needs new pipe. A pattern does. Plumbers look for several of the following together.

  • Repeated pinhole leaks in different rooms.
  • Rust-brown or yellow water when you first open a tap.
  • Low pressure at several fixtures that does not clear after cleaning aerators.
  • Green crust or stains on exposed copper, or flaking on older steel pipe.
  • Water stains on ceilings or walls that return after a patch.
  • A rising bill with no obvious fixture leak.

Confirm that the problem is in the pipes, not a single fixture. Our leak detection service can map the trouble spots first.

Pipe materials compared

Three materials are common today. Each has trade-offs, and the right one depends on the house and your budget. The notes below are general plumbing knowledge, not findings about Marina homes.

MaterialStrengthsTrade-offs
PEXFlexible, fewer fittings, often easier to run through finished wallsNot for outdoor sun exposure, and needs correct fittings
CopperLong track record, rigid and fire resistantMore labor, higher material cost, can pinhole in some water
CPVCLower cost and easy to joinCan turn brittle with age and heat

General knowledge: older homes in the United States sometimes have galvanized steel, which corrodes from the inside, or polybutylene, a gray plastic pipe used from the 1970s to the mid-1990s that is known for failures. Neither is a Marina finding. A plumber confirms what is in your walls by looking.

Repair a section or repipe the house

Repair is cheaper today. Repiping is cheaper if repairs keep coming. The decision comes down to pattern and condition.

SituationLikely best moveWhy
One leak, pipe otherwise soundRepair the sectionLittle reason to replace sound pipe
Pinholes in two or three roomsReplace the affected runs, or repipeFailures usually spread along the same pipe
Pipe known to be failing, like polybutyleneRepipeReplacement removes the risk across the house
Leaks under the slabReroute overhead or repipeCovered in slab leak repair

A sudden break is a different case. See burst pipe repair for shutoff steps and quick fixes.

What repiping typically costs

The same guide puts PEX at about $0.50 to $0.75 per foot for material and copper at $2 to $4. Home size, number of fixtures, wall and ceiling access, pipe material and patching drive the price. Finish repairs are often quoted separately, so ask what is included before you compare bids.

Marina water, permits and licensing

Marina Coast Water District pumps groundwater from wells and disinfects it with chlorine. In 2025 the district reported average total hardness of 10.54 grains per gallon, with a range of about 4.9 to 19.3. Hardness is not a regulated standard, but it means scale can build up in water heaters and fixtures, so new pipe does not replace regular flushing.

The district reports that all service lines in its area were verified as non-lead, and its inventory found no galvanized service lines. That covers the service connection only and says nothing about pipes inside a house. Whether a repipe needs a city permit should be confirmed with the Marina Building Division at (831) 884-1241.

California requires a contractor license for projects totaling $1,000 or more. A repipe is well above that. Check any license with the state board at 1-800-321-2752. About 57% of Marina homes are not owner-occupied by Census figures, so renters should ask their landlord rather than hire work themselves.

What the work looks like

  1. Walkthrough

    The plumber checks pipe material, shutoff valves, water pressure and access points, and counts fixtures.

  2. Written scope and price

    You get the material, the number of access openings and what is included in patching.

  3. Shutoff schedule

    Water is off for part of the job. Ask for the plan so you can arrange showers, laundry and cooking.

  4. New lines installed

    Old lines are cut out and new pipe is run through walls, attics or crawl spaces.

  5. Pressure test and patch

    The system is tested before walls close. Drywall patching is finished as agreed.

If you are also planning a heater or fixture upgrade, ask about doing it at the same time through plumbing installation.
