Until we arrive, shut the cold water valve above the tank, cut power at the breaker or turn the gas control off, and keep the area dry.

Tank failed and water is spreading: shut it down first

A steel tank that has corroded through cannot be patched. Water may pool slowly at the base or arrive in a rush. In both cases the first job is to stop the refill and remove the power source.

  • Cold water shutoff valve: closed, handle turned clockwise to stop.
  • Electric heater: breaker off. Gas heater: control knob to off.
  • One hot tap open upstairs to relieve pressure.
  • Items moved off the floor around the tank, and a wet vacuum or towels ready.
  • Standing water near outlets or a power strip: stay out and cut power from the panel only if the panel is dry.

Gas smell means leave

If you smell gas, go outside, do not use switches, and call 911 from a safe distance. Then call your gas utility. PG&E's line is 1-800-743-5000. Call us after the area is cleared.

Marina homes often keep the heater in a garage or closet. Water there can run under a wall into living space, so check the neighboring rooms. Tell us what you see and we will plan the visit around it.

Signs your heater is beyond repair

Not every failure means a new heater. Repair makes sense for a bad element, thermostat or thermocouple. Replacement makes sense when the tank itself is the problem or the unit is near the end of its life. National guides say conventional tanks often last about 8 to 12 years.

  • Water seeping from the tank body or the bottom seam.
  • Rust-colored hot water that clears on the cold side.
  • Loud rumbling from heavy scale buildup.
  • Repeat repairs within a short time.
  • A tank older than a decade with a new fault.

When the fault is a single part, see our water heater repair page. We will not sell a new tank when a small repair does the job.

How an emergency replacement works

  1. Diagnose

    We confirm the leak source and check the gas line, vent, relief valve pipe and water connections.

  2. Choose and price

    We go over fuel, size and type with you. You hear a clear price before work starts.

  3. Remove and set

    The old tank is drained and removed. The new one is placed on a pan or stand with fresh connections.

  4. Safety parts

    We fit a relief valve and discharge pipe, check for an expansion tank where the system calls for one, and strap the tank.

  5. Test

    We fire or power the unit, check for leaks and gas tightness, and confirm hot water at the taps.

Sizing, venting and access can change the schedule. Tight closets, attic spaces and gas vents need more time than a straight swap in a garage.

Gas, electric, tankless or heat pump when time is short

In an emergency, the quickest path is usually a like-for-like swap. A gas tank replaced with a gas tank keeps the existing gas line and vent. An electric tank replaced with electric keeps the circuit.

Switching type takes more planning. A tankless water heater may need a larger gas line, new venting or an electrical upgrade. A heat pump unit needs clearance and a condensate drain. State and local rules on replacement types have been changing, and we do not guess at them. Ask the City of Marina Building Division at (831) 884-1241 what applies to your address.

Bracing, permits and code questions in Marina

California requires new and replacement water heaters to be braced, anchored or strapped against earthquake motion under Health and Safety Code 19211, and the California Plumbing Code points to the same requirement. Typical guidance puts straps in the upper and lower thirds of the tank, with the lower strap at least 4 inches above the controls. A strapped tank is far less likely to tip and tear its gas or water lines in a quake.

Replacement usually needs a plumbing permit in California cities, but we do not state Marina's rule as fact. Call the Building Division at (831) 884-1241 to confirm. We also check the relief valve, its discharge pipe and thermal expansion on every install, since pressure problems shorten tank life.

Hard water and the life of the next heater

Marina Coast Water District's 2025 report shows hardness averaging about 10.5 grains per gallon, with a range of 4.9 to 19.3, and the water is chlorine-disinfected. The district suggests flushing your water heater regularly according to the manufacturer's guidance.

A new tank starts clean, so a yearly flush keeps scale from building up on the bottom. If you want to cut scale at the source, read about water softener and filtration options.
