Signs of a hidden leak

Hidden leaks usually announce themselves quietly. People call when the bill creeps up, when they hear a faint hiss at night, or when a patch of floor feels warm or damp. A leak can also show up as low pressure, a musty smell in a closet, or paint that bubbles on an outside wall.

  • The water meter keeps moving after every faucet, toilet and appliance is off.
  • A bill that climbs month after month with no change in how you use water.
  • A sound of running or hissing water in a quiet house.
  • Warm spots on a floor, which can point to a hot water line.
  • Stained drywall, bubbling paint, swollen baseboards or a musty smell.
  • Wet soil, a soft patch of lawn or a puddle near the meter box.

Check your meter before you call

A five-minute meter test tells you whether the leak is real and whether it sits inside the house or out in the yard. It costs nothing and gives the plumber a head start.

  1. Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation timer. Do not flush any toilet.
  2. Open the meter box lid and look at the dial. Many meters have a small leak indicator that spins at low flow.
  3. Write down the reading, wait 15 minutes, and look again. Any movement means water is going somewhere.
  4. Close the main valve at the house and check again. If the meter still moves, the leak is likely between the meter and the house. If it stops, the leak is inside.

Worth knowing

Do not pry at the meter or its box. The water district owns both. If the box itself is leaking or the meter looks damaged, call the Marina Coast Water District 24-hour line at (831) 883-5900.

Leak emergency: what to do before we arrive

Call (855) 575-2890 at any hour. The 24/7 line takes leak calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and we book same-day appointments when available. Until a plumber reaches you, your job is to limit the damage.

  • Shut the main water valve if water is actively escaping.
  • Switch off power to the wet room at the breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  • Move rugs, boxes and furniture off wet floors, and open cabinet doors under sinks to let air in.
  • Take photos of the damage and write down the meter reading before and after you close the valve.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building, call 911 from outside, then call PG&E at 1-800-743-5000. Do not flip switches.

Local factors: MCWD, the meter and your side of the line

Marina Coast Water District supplies Marina water from groundwater wells. Under the district's code, the district owns the meter and the service connection and maintains the meter and its box. The property owner is responsible for pipes and fixtures on the customer side of the meter. In practice, a leak before the meter is the district's to fix, and a leak after it is yours.

Timing matters. The district's conservation rules say plumbing and irrigation leaks must be repaired within 72 hours. The code also says no allowance is made for water lost through faulty fixtures, except where a leak happened without negligence by the customer, in which case the district may allow a credit. Treat any credit as discretionary, never as a promise. Get the leak found and fixed first.

The district also hands out free water-saving devices, including toilet leak detection tablets, through (831) 883-5905. A tablet test is a quick way to rule out a silently running toilet before you pay for a full search. If the meter test points to the yard, see water service line repair.
