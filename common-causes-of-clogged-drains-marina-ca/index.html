Common Causes of Clogged Drains in Marina, CA

Four culprits explain most slow drains and backups, and each one leaves a different clue.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Marina 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026.

The most common causes of clogged drains in Marina, CA come down to four things. Wipes and other items that do not belong in a toilet. Cooking grease in the kitchen line. Hair and soap in tubs and bathroom sinks. Roots in the pipe that runs from your house to the street. Each one leaves a different pattern, and reading that pattern tells you what to try first.

One slow sink usually means a clog close to that fixture. Two or three drains slowing at the same time usually means the main line. This guide walks through each cause, how to spot it, and when it is time to stop and call (855) 575-2890. Our line is open 24 hours a day if a drain backs up at night.

Wipes: the clog that looks harmless

Wipes are the top offender in toilet and main line clogs. Packages say "flushable," but that word describes whether the wipe fits through the bowl, not whether it breaks down in the pipe. Wipes hold together, snag on rough spots, and collect toilet paper and grease around them until the line is blocked.

The Marina Coast Water District (MCWD) says to flush only human waste and toilet paper. It also asks residents not to flush unapproved wipes because they can clog sewer lines and pipes. Paper towels, cotton swabs, dental floss and feminine products belong in the trash for the same reason.

Worth knowing

A toilet that drains slowly after every flush, or gurgles when a nearby shower runs, often has a wipe wad sitting just past the trap. A plunger sometimes moves it. If it only moves a few feet down the line, the clog returns.

Grease and cooking fat in kitchen lines

Grease goes down the sink as a hot liquid and turns into a waxy coating as it cools. Each wash adds a layer. Food scraps stick to that coating until the pipe narrows and the sink drains slowly, then stops.

An EPA report to Congress from 2004 found that grease from restaurants, homes and industrial sources was the most common cause of reported sanitary sewer overflows, at 47 percent of blockages. That is a national figure from a government source, and it shows how often grease is behind sewer trouble.

MCWD says hot water and soap do not eliminate fats, oils and grease because it can reform and solidify in the pipes. Running the tap hot after pouring bacon fat down the drain only moves the problem further down the line. Let fat cool, scrape it into the trash, and wipe pans with a paper towel before washing.

  • Never pour cooking oil, bacon fat or gravy down the sink or disposal.
  • Scrape plates into the trash before rinsing.
  • Use a sink strainer to catch food scraps.
  • Wipe greasy pans with a paper towel first.

Hair and soap in tubs, showers and bathroom sinks

Hair is the main reason tubs and showers clog. It wraps around the drain crossbar and the pop-up stopper, then catches soap scum and forms a mat. HomeAdvisor's 2025 cost guide says the same thing in plain terms: bathtubs and showers primarily collect hair and soap scum.

This is the clog most worth fixing yourself. Pull the stopper, clean off the hair, and use a drain strainer afterward. A cheap plastic drain snake often clears what is left. Skip harsh store-bought cleaners if you can, because plumbers warn they can corrode older pipes and are hard to handle safely.

Do baking soda and vinegar work?

They can loosen a minor clog because the fizz may push out small debris. Plumbing companies generally say they do little against a real clog of grease, hair and soap. Treat the mix as light maintenance, not a repair, and use a plunger or snake if water still stands in the basin.

Roots and trouble in the side sewer

Roots find tiny gaps at pipe joints, grow toward the moisture, and fill the pipe with a fine web that catches paper and waste. A franchise plumbing blog from 2026 calls tree roots the leading cause of blockages in water and sewer mains. That is one company's view, but it matches what plumbers see in older clay and cast iron lines.

Root clogs return. You clear the line, it works for a few months, then it slows again. The usual signs are a main line that backs up again and again, gurgling toilets, and a wet or unusually green patch in the yard along the sewer path.

A camera inspection shows whether roots, a bellied pipe or a cracked joint is the cause. That tells you whether cleaning is enough or the pipe needs repair. Our sewer inspection and sewer line repair pages explain both steps.

MCWD lists multiple interior drains backing up or clogged at the same time as a warning sign of a larger problem. If that is what you are seeing, do not keep running the dishwasher or washing machine. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.

What to try first and what to leave alone

A plunger, a hand snake and a cleaned trap solve most single-fixture clogs. Chemical drain openers are the thing to leave alone. They can sit in a stopped pipe, burn skin, and make the next plumber's work more hazardous.

  • Use the right plunger: a cup plunger for sinks, a flange plunger for toilets.
  • Remove and clean the P-trap under the sink with a bucket underneath.
  • Pour no chemicals into standing water.
  • Stop if the water rises or you hear gurgling from other drains.

What MCWD and Marina's code say about your sewer line

Marina Coast Water District owns and operates the pipes and pumps that carry wastewater to the Monterey One Water Regional Treatment Plant. That covers the public sewer in the street. The pipe from your house to that public line, called the side sewer or lateral, is a different matter.

According to MCWD's code, section 5.16.090, side sewers are maintained by the owner of the property served. In plain terms, a clog or crack in your lateral is yours to clear and repair, not the district's. The district's wastewater page asks owners to look after their laterals.

The same code, section 5.16.120, calls for cleanouts at the plumbing connection, at bends of 45 degrees or more, and at the lateral connection, spaced no more than 100 feet apart. Know where your cleanout cap is. It is the access point for cleaning and for a camera, and a missing or buried one adds work.

MCWD's grease program and the rule requiring grease traps apply to food service businesses, not homes. Even so, the district's message about fats, oils and grease applies to every kitchen. If you see sewage coming back up, MCWD asks you to report a sewer backup or spill at (831) 384-6131 or (831) 883-5900.
