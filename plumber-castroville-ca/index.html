A town, not a city: what that means for your plumbing

Castroville is a census-designated place in Monterey County, not an incorporated city. The 2020 Census counted 7,515 people there. Your ZIP is 95012, and you have no city hall, so the Castroville Community Services District handles water, sewer and other local services.

The practical result is that a street-side problem goes to the district, while a problem inside your property line is yours. District offices are at 11499 Geil Street, phone (831) 633-2560. We repair the pipes you own, and we tell you plainly when the fault is on the district's side so you do not pay for the wrong repair.

Census figures for 2020 to 2024 show 45.5 percent of Castroville homes are owner-occupied, so many households rent. If you rent, shut off the water, tell your landlord, then call us with their contact. Our drain cleaning work serves owners and managers alike.

Rain season, low ground and sewer backups

Castroville sits near Elkhorn Slough and Moro Cojo Slough. News reports from March 12 and 13, 2023 said the Sheriff's Office issued evacuation warnings for low-lying parts of Castroville after heavy rain. We cite this only as reported, but it shows why a wet winter deserves a plan.

During long storms, a sewer lateral can slow down, and a yard drain can overflow near the foundation. Know where your cleanout is, keep it clear, and call early rather than after the water reaches the floor.

Water from the district: hardness and what it does to fixtures

Castroville Community Services District serves more than 7,000 customers through 2,145 water connections. According to its 2025 report, the water comes from wells in the 400-foot and 900-foot aquifers. Average hardness was 145 ppm, with a range of 25 to 243 ppm. As a rough conversion, 145 mg/L is about 8.5 grains per gallon, which counts as moderately hard.

The same report lists total dissolved solids at 527 ppm on average and nitrate at 0.6 ppm. For you, hardness is the number that matters most. It leaves white scale on shower heads and faucet screens, and it builds up inside a water heater tank. Flushing a heater once a year slows that buildup.

If scale keeps clogging aerators or fixtures, ask us about a water softener and filtration setup. Samples ranged widely, so test your own tap before you buy any equipment.

Pipes, leaks and the water line to the street

Castroville's homes come from many eras, and we do not quote a median year because the figures we found came from secondary sources. One house may have copper and the next galvanized steel, so we check your pipes rather than assume.

A sudden jump in your bill, a damp patch in the yard or a meter that keeps moving with every tap off points to a hidden leak. Our leak detection visit traces it with sound and moisture tools before we dig. When the leak is in the line from the meter to the house, a water service line repair is the fix.

Gas smell? Do not wait for a plumber

Leave the building, call 911 from a safe distance, and then call your gas utility. We do not promise a response time for gas leaks, and a plumber is never the first call for one.

Farm-town businesses and the Artichoke Center nickname

Castroville is nicknamed the Artichoke Center of the World, and the town still depends on food, farming and the shops that serve them. Restaurants, packing sheds and small stores all share one concern, which is grease and solids in the drain line.

Food-service kitchens in the Marina Coast Water District area follow a grease program under that district's code. Castroville's own district rules may differ, so ask the district for yours. Our commercial plumbing crews can plan routine cleanings around your service hours.
