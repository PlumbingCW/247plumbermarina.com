Main Sewer Line Repair Cost in Marina, CA

What the published guides say, what they leave out, and what the owner is responsible for in Marina.

Call (855) 575-2890

By the Marina 24/7 Plumbing team · Published October 29, 2026 · Updated October 29, 2026.

Main sewer line repair cost in Marina, CA depends mostly on how the pipe is reached and how long the damaged run is. Published national guides in 2025 and 2026 put full replacement at roughly $1,300 to $5,300, with averages near $3,200 to $3,300. Spot repairs of a cracked section are shown at about $600 to $1,050. These are national figures, not Marina quotes.

Most cost guides online are national, and we say so plainly. We found no published Central Coast price range, so the numbers below are context for judging a quote, not a prediction. According to Marina Coast Water District code, the property owner maintains the side sewer, so the bill is usually yours. If your drains are backing up now, call (855) 575-2890.

Why one job costs three times another

The price is driven by access and method more than by the pipe itself. A short run reached from a cleanout in an open yard is a different job from a long run under a driveway or a house slab.

  • Length and depth of the damaged run. Guides show $60 to $250 per linear foot for trenchless work and $50 to $200 per foot for traditional excavation.
  • What sits on top. HomeAdvisor shows work under a slab adding $300 to $350 per foot, and restoring concrete or landscaping runs $1 to $25 per square foot.
  • Method. Pipe lining is shown at $90 to $250 per foot, pipe bursting at $60 to $200 per foot.
  • Permits. Guides show about $200 on HomeAdvisor and $100 to $1,000 on This Old House.

Ask any contractor which method they propose and why. Trenchless is not always possible, and a bellied or collapsed pipe can force a dig.

Ground matters too. A state environmental document describes the Marina area dunes as unconsolidated, highly erodible sand. For you, that means trench walls can slump, so ask how a contractor plans to support the trench and keep the neighbors' fences and walkways safe. Nothing in the guides prices this, which is another reason a national average is only a starting point.

Camera first, then a decision

A camera inspection shows where the line is cracked, offset, root-filled or collapsed. Guides show it at $100 to $500. That is a small fraction of a replacement and can save you from paying for a repair you did not need.

Sometimes the answer is cleaning, not repair. HomeAdvisor shows clearing a main line clog at $100 to $800, and roots at $100 to $600. If the camera shows a sound pipe with roots, cleaning on a schedule may cost far less than replacement. Our sewer inspection and sewer line cleaning pages explain both steps.

Signs the main line is failing

  • More than one drain backing up at the same time, such as a tub gurgling when the washer drains.
  • Slow drains throughout the house that return days after snaking.
  • Sewage smell in the yard or garage, or a wet, sunken patch over the line.
  • Water coming out of a floor drain or the lowest fixture in the house.

Stop using water when you see these. Every flush and shower adds to what has nowhere to go.

Sewer backup: what to do first

  1. Stop the water

    Stop laundry, dishes, showers and flushing. Keep children and pets away from standing sewage.

  2. Call for help

    Call (855) 575-2890. We answer 24 hours a day, and we offer same-day appointments when available.

  3. Report it

    If sewage reaches the street or you are unsure whose pipe it is, tell the water district. The numbers are in the Marina notes below.

  4. Protect the room

    Move belongings off the floor and ventilate. Do not touch wet outlets.

Marina notes: who owns the pipe and who to call

Marina Coast Water District code section 5.16.090 says side sewers shall be maintained by the owner of the property served. The side sewer, also called the lateral, runs from your house to the district sewer main. For you, that means a cracked lateral is a homeowner repair, not a district repair, so budget for it and get a camera look before you commit.

MCWD code also requires cleanouts at the plumbing connection, at bends of 45 degrees or more, and at the lateral connection, with spacing not over 100 feet (section 5.16.120). For you, a cleanout is the access point that keeps inspection and cleaning cheap. If your yard has no cleanout, a plumber can often add one, and that makes future work simpler.

To report a sewer backup or spill, MCWD lists (831) 384-6131 and (831) 883-5900. The second line is staffed around the clock for emergencies. The district also warns that several interior drains backing up at once points to a larger problem. Report it, then call a plumber for your side of the line.

MCWD's plumbing code also requires plumbers working under its jurisdiction to hold a state contractor license and file insurance with the district. When you hire, ask for the CSLB license number and check it at 1-800-321-CSLB. We serve homes in Seaside and Castroville as well, where the sewer provider may differ, so confirm your own district first.
