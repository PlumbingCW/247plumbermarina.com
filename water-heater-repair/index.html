No hot water or a leaking tank: what to do first

Water seeping from the base of the jacket usually means the steel tank has rusted through, and that cannot be patched. A drip at the pipes on top, or at the relief valve, is often a fixable part.

  1. Close the cold water shutoff valve on the pipe above the heater. Turn the handle clockwise until it stops.
  2. Electric unit: switch off its breaker. Gas unit: turn the gas control knob to off or pilot.
  3. Open one hot tap in the house so the system can vent while the tank cools.
  4. Move boxes and stored items away from the base, and put a bucket under any steady drip.
  5. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us the fuel type, the tank size on the label, and where the water is coming from.

If you smell gas

Leave the house, do not touch switches or light anything, and call 911 from a safe distance. Then call your gas utility. In PG&E territory that number is 1-800-743-5000. Do not try to relight a pilot until the smell is gone and the source is found.

A drain pan under the tank buys only a few minutes. Shutting the cold valve stops the refill and limits the damage.

Gas heaters: pilot light and thermocouple problems

On a standing-pilot gas heater, a thermocouple is a safety sensor. It detects the pilot flame and lets gas flow to the burner. When it wears out, it shuts the gas off even if the pilot looks lit, which is why a pilot that drops out after a few minutes often points to this part.

A thermocouple swap is a short job, and published cost guides put it near $150 when a plumber does it. If the pilot still will not hold, the cause may be a bad gas control valve or a blocked vent, and that needs a professional.

  • A rotten-egg smell means leave first and call 911, then the gas utility.
  • Carbon monoxide alarms near gas appliances are worth testing. A CO alarm senses carbon monoxide, not natural gas.

Electric and hybrid heat pump heater repairs

Standard electric heaters use two heating elements and two thermostats. If you get lukewarm water, the lower element is often the one that failed. If you get none, the upper element, a thermostat, or the high-limit reset may be at fault.

Hybrid heat pump heaters add a compressor, a fan and a condensate drain on top of the tank. Typical problems include a clogged air filter, a blocked condensate line, or a fault code that sends the unit into backup electric mode.

Bring up the brand and model when you call. If you have a heat pump unit and are asking about a swap, ask the City of Marina Building Division at (831) 884-1241 how current rules apply to your situation. We do not guess at code on the phone.

Marina water, scale and the anode rod

Marina Coast Water District reports moderately hard to hard water. Its 2025 water quality report shows total hardness averaging about 10.5 grains per gallon, ranging from 4.9 to 19.3, and the district disinfects with chlorine. District guidance suggests flushing the water heater regularly, following the maker's instructions, to limit mineral buildup.

That matters at your tank. Hard water drops minerals onto the bottom and onto heating elements, which causes rumbling and early element failure.

The anode rod is a metal rod inside the tank that corrodes so the steel does not. Guides suggest checking it every few years. A replacement runs about $50 to $150 including labor in published guides, and it can add years to a sound tank. A tank that is already leaking cannot be saved this way.

Repair or replace? An honest look at the trade-offs

Tank heaters often last about 8 to 12 years, per national cost guides. A younger unit with a single failed part is usually worth repairing. An older tank with a leak, heavy rust or repeat failures is usually not. We will tell you which side your unit falls on and show you both prices.
